- **A natural pairing**: Operations (the new 4-player co-op mode, live since June 30, 2026) fits the squadron's own "no piracy, no griefing" identity well — arriving at Rigel and running an Operations session or two with active squadron members would combine the trip with something concretely new to try, rather than just a scenic visit.

This round, at the user's prompt, re-examined the whole six-system chain's actual current political map on Inara/EDSM rather than treating it as undifferentiated logistics grind — and it turns out to already split cleanly into two fronts, one of which is a genuine, currently-live BGS opportunity, not just a narrative label.

That's two clusters, not one chain: a **far cluster** (IH-V/VF-N/WC-X, all SJC, ~50–60 ly apart from each other, ~1,150–1,330 ly out near the new colonisation frontier — and, usefully, only ~862 ly from Rigel itself, roughly the same neighborhood as item 1's destination) and a **near cluster right beside Phiagre** (Shui Wei, 8.6 ly away, also already SJC — literally a satellite of the old home).

**The "first system" question, answered honestly:** the literal first system claimed, Shui Wei, is *already* Sovereign Justice Collective territory — not a rebellion target, a beachhead. **The two systems that actually fit "seed a rebellion assisted by SJC" are the other two in the near cluster — DZ-J and OX-B** — and here's the part worth knowing before deciding anything: **SJC already has a real, live foothold in both, right now:**

- **Col 285 Sector OX-B b13-1** — controlled by **Jormungand Empire Group** (Empire allegiance, Patronage government) at 61.1% influence, followed by HIP 11192 Order (19.2%). **The Sovereign Justice Collective sits third at 13.8% influence, and is in an active Expansion state** `[EDSM factions data]` — not a hypothetical presence, an actual ongoing BGS push.
- **Col 285 Sector DZ-J b24-0** — controlled by **Talon Security** (Independent, Corporate government) at 59.3%, followed by Division Cassini (20.3%). **SJC sits third at 14.0% influence, also actively Expanding** `[EDSM factions data]`.

**Motives an SJC-assisted breakaway would actually have, given what these two governments are:**

- **OX-B is Imperial Patronage** — hereditary-nobility, favor-and-obligation rule, the Empire's classic aristocratic government type. A justice-and-vigilance-branded collective backing a populist break from an Imperial patron's Empire-aligned rule is about as clean a motive as Elite's political flavor offers — and OX-B is the largest prize of the two by population (307,000).
- **DZ-J is a Corporate security firm ruling its own system** — "Talon Security" holding Corporate government over ~96,000 people reads as a private security contractor that became the government, which sits in genuine tension with a squadron whose whole motto is about who actually keeps the peace and for whom. A "we don't need a security corporation charging rent on vigilance, we already do vigilance" angle writes itself.

**How this serves the squadron's larger picture:** it's a second front, geographically distinct from item 1's Rigel trip — contribution near the *old* home rather than the new one, and one where SJC is already committed (Expansion state in both systems means the faction is already actively trying to grow there; a player pushing the same direction — bounty-hunting/trading/mission-running for SJC, or actively undermining the current controller — accelerates something already underway rather than starting cold).

**Resource backing / fallback, concretely:** Phiagre itself (70M population, Medium security, Industrial/Extraction economy, SJC-controlled, `Depleted` reserve — a mature, settled system) and Shui Wei (the 8.6-ly satellite, ~199,000 population, Service economy, also SJC) sit **only ~205–242 ly from both OX-B and DZ-J** — a short, practical logistics/fallback base. By contrast, the far IH-V/VF-N/WC-X cluster (the actual "home" today) is **~1,150–1,330 ly away from OX-B/DZ-J** — much too far to serve as a staging point for this. In other words: **the old home, not the new one, is the natural base of operations for this specific campaign.**

**What this doesn't resolve, on purpose:** neither system shows an active Civil War/rebellion state yet — both are simply "Expansion," meaning any actual flip would need real BGS effort (missions/trade/bounty-hunting for SJC, and working against the current controller) sustained over time, the normal way faction control changes in Elite. Nothing here is instant, and this is offered as the shape/motive for that grind, not a shortcut around it.

## 6. Powerplay 2.0 — pledge as a means to specific modules, not an end in itself

Reconsidered this round, and reconsidered again after the user's point below about ship modernity — the two aren't independent decisions.

The earlier version of this list left Powerplay off entirely, reasoning that nine years of a clean Independent stance was itself a consistent identity choice. That's still true as *history* — but it doesn't have to be a permanent constraint, and the 2.0 rework changes the actual trade being offered: merits no longer decay, so progress is permanent rather than a grind treadmill, and each Power unlocks a set of exclusive reward modules along the way (all 12 modules become available to every Power eventually, around rank 97+, but the unlock order and speed differ by Power) `[external: Inara Powerplay 2.0 logbook guide, forum discussion]`.

**Before spending merits on an older hull, there needs to be a real reason it's the older hull and not a newer one already sitting in the fleet.** The user's point, and it holds up: ships released alongside or after Supercruise Overcharge (SCO) — the FSD variant that lets a ship boost through supercruise for much faster long-distance travel — come **pre-equipped and optimized for it** (better heat/fuel/handling under SCO than an older hull retrofitted with the same drive) `[external: SCO guide, community sources]`. The known SCO-optimized hull types are: Cobra MkV, Mandalay, Type-8, Python Mk II, Imperial Corsair, Type-11 Prospector, and Panther Clipper MkII. **Checked against the actual fleet (properly, by `ShipID` rather than by name — see the sourcing note below on why that distinction mattered here): every one of these seven hull types is already owned** `[data, this round]` — Cobra MkV ("Master of None"), Mandalay ("Quo Vadis"), Type-8 (five hulls), Python Mk II ("Nepo Baby," "High Gravitas Warning"), Imperial Corsair ("Reignmaker" and one unnamed), Panther Clipper MkII ("Worth The Weight"), and the Type-11 Prospector (see below). So this fleet is not behind on modern hulls — the real question for item 6 is why the Federation Corvette and FerDeLance (both older, non-SCO-optimized designs) are the ones earmarked for Powerplay module investment, when SCO-optimized combat-capable hulls (the Corsair especially) are already owned and could plausibly take over the same role with better travel characteristics. **This list doesn't resolve that for you** — the Corvette's combat record (1,524 real kills) is a genuinely strong case for *it* specifically regardless of hull age, so "newer" isn't automatically "better" for an established role — but it's the right test to apply before committing merits: is there a specific reason (proven track record, role fit, weapon-slot layout) the older ship should get the investment, or is that just where the ship happened to be already?

**A more concrete finding from checking this: the Type-11 Prospector was already tried, not left unflown.** It was bought and flown across 10 real sessions between 29 Sep and 28 Nov 2025 (75 events), under the name "Mine Now" — then apparently set aside in favor of returning to the original Type-8 (also named "Mine Now" at various points, which is what caused the confusion below). It's still owned, currently sitting in storage at **Col 132 Sector VF-N d7-64** — one of the item-2 colony systems — as of the most recent fleet snapshot (7 June 2026) `[data]`. Given mining is this fleet's best-performing activity per hour (per the deep-dive round's economy findings), and a dedicated mining hull has already been bought and set aside rather than never tried, **revisiting why it was shelved and whether it's worth bringing back into rotation** is a more specific, better-grounded goal than "try a new ship" — the ship isn't new to this commander, it's underused.

Framed as a means to an end — specific modules for specific ships already doing specific jobs, not "grinding Powerplay for its own sake" — a few concrete pairings stand out:

- **The Federation Corvette "Battle of Grunwald" is, by a wide margin, this commander's real bounty-hunting workhorse**: 1,524 of the 2,531 lifetime `FactionKillBond` awards trace back to it, all confirmed as genuine ship-to-ship kills (not SRV/on-foot, see the correction below), dwarfing every other ship in the fleet `[data, this round]`. A PP combat weapon suited to sustained bounty work — the **Retributor** beam laser (spikes target heat rather than raw damage — a "wear them down" tool that fits the Sovereign-Justice-Collective vigilance identity better than a bigger gun would) or the **Pacifier Frag-Cannons** (extended range, tighter pellet spread — a straightforwardly better close-range brawling weapon) — would be a direct, evidence-based upgrade to the ship that's already doing the bulk of the work, not a speculative one. (Not SCO-optimized — see the modernity note above; the case for investing here anyway is the 1,524-kill track record, not the hull.)
- **Quo Vadis (the Mandalay explorer, and the ship making the Rigel trip in item 1) is a strong candidate for Prismatic Shields** — a reward module that gives a shield generator the strength of the next size up, at extra mass/power cost `[external]`. That's a meaningful survivability boost for a long-range exploration ship that (per the Surface Activity round) already turns up in surface conflict zones and interdictions incidentally, without exploring's usual class-appropriate shield being upsized outright. Mandalay is itself SCO-optimized, so this pairing doesn't run into the modernity question at all. **Aisling Duval is reportedly the fastest route to it — rank 34, roughly 247,000 merits, versus rank 50+ for other Imperial Powers and rank 57+ for Winters** `[external, forum discussion — worth a quick in-game/Inara re-check before committing, since unlock-order specifics like this tend to shift between balance passes]`.
- **The FerDeLance "mistake not" is the fleet's real second ship-to-ship combat vessel** — 167 `FactionKillBond` awards earned by the ship's own weapons `[data, corrected this round]`. (An earlier pass at this ranking wrongly credited the DiamondbackXL "Heart of Gold" with 209 kills and called it the clear #2 bounty-hunting ship — the user directly questioned that finding, and re-checking vehicle state at the moment of each kill found 208 of those 209 were actually earned in the SRV and 1 on foot, all in one marathon settlement-raiding session, with Heart of Gold sitting on the ground the whole time as the mothership. It contributed zero ship-to-ship kills — see the sourcing notes below for the full correction and what it exposed about the attribution method, and item 5 above for what Heart of Gold is actually good for instead.) The **Cytoscramblers** (a class-1 burst laser dealing class-3 damage specifically against shields, at reduced range) would suit the FerDeLance's fast, aggressive combat role well. (Also not SCO-optimized — same modernity caveat as the Corvette; the FerDeLance's own real combat record is the justification, not the hull's age.)
- **The colonization hauling fleet (the Type-8s and Type-9 doing the item-2 supply runs) is the one place where a defensive PP module might matter more than an offensive one**: the **Containment Missile** (reboots a target's FSD on impact, at lower raw damage than a standard missile) is built for exactly the "stop a pursuer from following, don't necessarily need to kill it" situation a slow, laden hauler runs into — and fits the squadron's own "no piracy, no griefing" ethos as a deterrent rather than an escalation. Type-8 is itself SCO-optimized, so this one's clean on the modernity question too.

Worth being explicit about the trade-off this actually involves, so it's a deliberate choice rather than a drift: pledging to a Power is a real commitment (rank progress, per-Power perks, a visible allegiance) that sits differently from the nine-year Independent stance documented in the backstory doc. Nothing here says which Power fits best beyond the Prismatic Shields unlock-speed note above — that's a call this list intentionally leaves to you, once the specific module(s) worth pledging for are chosen.

## Sourcing / method notes

- Colony-chain dates and systems, StarType counts, and Guardian-module ownership are all direct `journal.duckdb` queries, re-verified this round.
- Rigel's coordinates came from EDSM (`edsm.net`), since the journal has no recorded visit of its own to draw them from; the ~862 ly figure is a straight-line distance, not a routed jump count.
- The "zero visits to Rigel" figure required catching and correcting a false-positive from an earlier round (a "Maidubrigel" substring match had been misread as "Rigel") — see `cmdr-hussar-backstory.md` for the full correction. Flagged here too since it directly affects how item 1 is framed.
- Powerplay 2.0 module list, effects, merit mechanics, and the Prismatic-Shields unlock-speed comparison are from Inara's Powerplay 2.0 logbook guide and a Frontier forums discussion thread (both external, not journal data) — these are exactly the kind of detail that can drift with balance patches, so worth a quick re-check in-game or on Inara before actually committing merits toward a specific module.
- Item 2's current controlling-faction/government/population/influence figures are from EDSM's public system and faction APIs (`edsm.net`), fetched this round — these reflect BGS state as of the lookup and will keep changing with ordinary play by everyone in those systems, not just this commander, so they're a snapshot to plan from, not a fixed fact to bank on.
- **A real methodology gap, caught and fixed this round**: the per-ship `FactionKillBond` attribution (ship = nearest-preceding `Loadout` event in the same session) correctly identifies which ship you were flying at the *start* of a combat streak, but doesn't check whether you were still actually in that ship — versus on foot or in an SRV — at the exact moment of each individual kill. This produced a real, reported-then-corrected error: the DiamondbackXL "Heart of Gold" was credited with 209 kills and called the fleet's #2 bounty-hunting ship, when 208 of those were actually SRV kills and 1 was on foot, all from a single settlement-raiding session where the DBX never fired a shot. The corrected method checks vehicle state (SHIP/SRV/FOOT, using the same LaunchSRV/DockSRV/Disembark/Embark boundary logic built for the Surface Activity report page) at each kill's timestamp before counting it toward a ship's combat total. Re-run against the whole fleet, this holds up for the Federation Corvette (1,524 kills, 100% genuine ship-to-ship) and surfaces the FerDeLance "mistake not" (167) as the real #2, not the DiamondbackXL. **Worth checking whether the same gap affects any other per-ship "combat" or "usage" numbers elsewhere in this project** (e.g. the Ship Profile Analysis fit-vs-usage table, or the Combat Analysis page) — not re-verified yet, flagged here so it isn't forgotten.
- **Heart of Gold's landing-pad/hardpoint note (item 5)**: DiamondbackXL landing pad size and hardpoint layout (1 Large + 2 Medium) are ship specs from Inara's ship-database page, not journal data (the journal doesn't record landing pad size). The specific weapon loadout quoted (Large gimballed beam laser, 2 Medium fixed dumbfire missile racks) is Heart of Gold's own latest `Loadout` event, confirmed directly from the database.
- **A second real methodology gap, caught this round while checking fleet-wide SCO coverage**: a quick "distinct ship types currently owned" query, grouped by `ShipName` rather than the stable `ShipID`, silently missed the Type-11 Prospector entirely — because its name ("Mine Now") had previously been used by an unrelated Type-8, and grouping by name let the Type-8's more-recent `Loadout` event win, hiding the Type-11 as if it didn't exist. Re-run grouped by `ShipID` (the same stable-identifier approach `journal_common.py`'s actual ship-attribution logic already uses, and validated against synthetic ground truth back in the deep-dive round) surfaced the full, correct 34-ship / 28-type fleet, including the Type-11 and three other owned types (`belugaliner`, `empire_trader`, `krait_light`) a name-based query would also have missed. This is the same underlying lesson as the DiamondbackXL kill-attribution gap above — a name is not a stable identifier in this data, `ShipID` is — just surfacing in a different kind of query (fleet inventory) rather than a per-event attribution. Caught before it was reported as a finding, not after, this time.
- SCO (Supercruise Overcharge) mechanics, the specific list of SCO-optimized ship types, and the Kestrel Mk II's specs are from external community/press sources (a Steam guide, ship-reveal press coverage), not journal data — Elite Dangerous balance and ship specs change over time, so these are worth a quick re-check before acting on them, same as the Powerplay module details above.
